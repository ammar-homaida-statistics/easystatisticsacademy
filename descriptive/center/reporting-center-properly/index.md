<section class="section">
  <div class="section-head">
    <h1>Reporting Center Properly</h1>
    <p class="lead">
      Computing a measure of center is not enough.
      You must report it correctly, transparently, and in alignment with the data type.
    </p>
  </div>

  <hr>

  <h2>1. Always Match the Measure to the Variable Type</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Nominal → Mode</li>
    <li>Ordinal → Median (primary)</li>
    <li>Interval/Ratio → Mean (if distribution is appropriate)</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Reporting an invalid measure reduces credibility.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>2. Report the Measure with Context</h2>

  <p>
    Do not report numbers in isolation.
  </p>

  <p><strong>Weak:</strong> “The mean is 72.”</p>

  <p><strong>Better:</strong> “The average exam score was 72 out of 100.”</p>

  <p>
    Always include:
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>Variable name</li>
    <li>Units</li>
    <li>Sample size (n)</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>3. Include Spread When Necessary</h2>

  <p>
    A measure of center without variability is incomplete.
  </p>

  <p>
    Examples:
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>Mean = 72, SD = 8</li>
    <li>Median = 4, IQR = 1</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Center + Spread gives a fuller description.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>4. Avoid Over-Precision</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Do not report unnecessary decimal places.</li>
    <li>Match precision to measurement accuracy.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Example:
  </p>

  <p>
    Mean income = 45,372.84291 → unnecessary precision  
    Better: 45,373 (or rounded appropriately)
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>5. Interpret Carefully</h2>

  <p>
    Measures of center describe data.
    They do not explain causes.
  </p>

  <p>
    Avoid:
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>“The high mean proves the intervention worked.”</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Descriptive statistics summarize — they do not infer.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>6. Example — Academic Reporting</h2>

  <p>
    “The average weekly study time was 12.4 hours (SD = 3.2, n = 80).”
  </p>

  <p>
    Or for ordinal:
  </p>

  <p>
    “The median satisfaction rating was 4 (Agree), with most responses clustered between 3 and 4.”
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>7. Checklist Before Reporting</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Is the measure valid for the scale?</li>
    <li>Is the unit clearly stated?</li>
    <li>Is sample size reported?</li>
    <li>Is variability included?</li>
    <li>Is interpretation descriptive (not causal)?</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>Summary</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Correct measure depends on measurement scale.</li>
    <li>Always include context and units.</li>
    <li>Combine center with spread.</li>
    <li>Avoid causal language.</li>
    <li>Clarity builds credibility.</li>
  </ul>
