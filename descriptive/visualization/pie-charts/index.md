<div class="section-head">
  <h1>Pie Charts (Limits & Proper Use)</h1>
  <p>
    Pie charts represent proportions of a whole.
    They are simple — but often misused.
  </p>
</div>

<hr>

<h2>1. When Pie Charts Are Appropriate</h2>

Use pie charts when:

<ul>
  <li>The variable is categorical</li>
  <li>You want to show parts of a whole</li>
  <li>There are few categories (ideally ≤ 5)</li>
  <li>Percentages sum to 100%</li>
</ul>

Example:
<ul>
  <li>Market share distribution</li>
  <li>Budget allocation</li>
</ul>

<hr>

<h2>2. What a Pie Chart Communicates</h2>

<p>
A pie chart emphasizes proportion.
</p>

<p>
Each slice represents:
</p>

<ul>
  <li>A percentage of the total</li>
  <li>A fraction of the whole circle</li>
</ul>

<p>
The entire circle equals 100%.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>3. Major Limitation: Angle Comparison Is Hard</h2>

<p>
Humans compare lengths better than angles.
</p>

<p>
This means:
</p>

<ul>
  <li>Small differences are hard to detect</li>
  <li>Close categories look visually similar</li>
</ul>

<p>
Bar charts are usually better for comparison.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>4. Too Many Categories Problem</h2>

<p>
Pie charts become unreadable when:
</p>

<ul>
  <li>There are many categories</li>
  <li>Some categories are very small</li>
</ul>

<p>
In such cases, use a bar chart instead.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>5. 3D Pie Charts (Avoid)</h2>

<p>
3D pie charts distort perception.
</p>

<p>
Slices closer to the viewer appear larger.
</p>

<p>
This creates visual bias.
</p>

<p>
Avoid 3D effects in statistical reporting.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>6. Pie Chart vs Bar Chart</h2>

<p>
Choose:
</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Pie chart</strong> → emphasize proportion of whole</li>
  <li><strong>Bar chart</strong> → emphasize comparison between categories</li>
</ul>

<p>
When in doubt, choose the bar chart.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>7. Interpreting a Pie Chart</h2>

When interpreting:

<ul>
  <li>Identify the largest slice</li>
  <li>Identify very small slices</li>
  <li>Avoid overstating minor differences</li>
  <li>Focus on overall proportion patterns</li>
</ul>

<hr>

<h2>Summary</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Pie charts show parts of a whole.</li>
  <li>Use only with few categories.</li>
  <li>Avoid 3D distortion.</li>
  <li>Prefer bar charts for precise comparison.</li>
</ul>

<p>
A pie chart is simple — but simplicity does not guarantee clarity.
</p>
