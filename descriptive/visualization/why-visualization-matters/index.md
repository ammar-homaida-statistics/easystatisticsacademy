<div class="section-head">
  <h1>Why Visualization Matters</h1>
  <p>
    Tables provide exact numbers.  
    Visualizations reveal patterns.
  </p>
</div>

<hr>

<h2>1. Humans Detect Patterns Visually</h2>

<p>
The human brain detects trends, clusters, and outliers much faster visually than numerically.
</p>

<p>
Consider two representations:
</p>

<ul>
  <li>A frequency table</li>
  <li>A bar chart of the same data</li>
</ul>

<p>
Both contain the same information.  
But the bar chart makes the dominant category immediately visible.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>2. Visualization Reduces Cognitive Load</h2>

<p>
A table may contain 20 numbers.
</p>

<p>
A graph compresses those 20 numbers into:
</p>

<ul>
  <li>Shape</li>
  <li>Height differences</li>
  <li>Position</li>
</ul>

<p>
This reduces mental effort and speeds interpretation.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>3. Visualization Reveals Distribution Shape</h2>

<p>
Tables show frequencies.
</p>

<p>
Graphs show:
</p>

<ul>
  <li>Skewness</li>
  <li>Symmetry</li>
  <li>Outliers</li>
  <li>Clusters</li>
</ul>

<p>
For numerical data, this is essential before calculating means or standard deviations.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>4. Visualization Helps Compare Groups</h2>

<p>
Graphs allow side-by-side comparison:
</p>

<ul>
  <li>Bar charts for categories</li>
  <li>Boxplots for distributions</li>
  <li>Scatterplots for relationships</li>
</ul>

<p>
Visual comparison prevents misinterpretation caused by reading numbers separately.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>5. But Visualization Can Mislead</h2>

<p>
Graphs are powerful — and power can distort.
</p>

Common issues:
<ul>
  <li>Truncated axes</li>
  <li>Unequal scaling</li>
  <li>3D distortion</li>
  <li>Cherry-picked ranges</li>
</ul>

<p>
A misleading graph can distort perception more than a misleading table.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>6. Tables First, Graphs Second</h2>

<p>
Best practice:
</p>

<ul>
  <li>Build a correct table first</li>
  <li>Then visualize responsibly</li>
</ul>

<p>
Visualization should never replace numerical understanding.  
It should support it.
</p>

<hr>

<h2>Summary</h2>

<ul>
  <li>Graphs reveal patterns faster than tables</li>
  <li>They reduce cognitive load</li>
  <li>They show distribution shape clearly</li>
  <li>They help compare groups</li>
  <li>They can mislead if poorly designed</li>
</ul>

<p>
Visualization is not decoration.  
It is structured statistical communication.
</p>
