<section class="section">
  <div class="section-head">
    <h1>Standard Table Layout & Reporting</h1>
    <p>
      A correct table is not only about numbers.  
      It must also be clearly labeled, transparent, and professionally presented.
    </p>
  </div>

  <h2>1. Every Table Must Have a Clear Title</h2>

  <p>
    A good table title:
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>States what variables are shown</li>
    <li>Indicates the population or sample</li>
    <li>Includes sample size (n)</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Example:
  </p>

  <p><strong>Table 1. Preferred Study Method by Gender (n = 40)</strong></p>

  <hr>

  <h2>2. Label Rows and Columns Clearly</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Use full words (avoid unclear abbreviations)</li>
    <li>Include units if applicable</li>
    <li>Indicate if percentages are row %, column %, or total %</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>3. Always Include Totals</h2>

  <p>
    Totals verify the denominator and allow the reader to audit the table.
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>Row totals</li>
    <li>Column totals</li>
    <li>Grand total</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Missing totals create ambiguity.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>4. Example of a Properly Formatted Table</h2>

  <p><strong>Table 1. Preferred Study Method by Gender (n = 40)</strong></p>

  <table class="table">
    <thead>
      <tr>
        <th>Gender</th>
        <th>Group Study (%)</th>
        <th>Individual Study (%)</th>
        <th>Total (n)</th>
      </tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr>
        <td>Male</td>
        <td>40%</td>
        <td>60%</td>
        <td>20</td>
      </tr>
      <tr>
        <td>Female</td>
        <td>70%</td>
        <td>30%</td>
        <td>20</td>
      </tr>
      <tr>
        <td><strong>Total</strong></td>
        <td>55%</td>
        <td>45%</td>
        <td><strong>40</strong></td>
      </tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>

  <p>
    Note: Row percentages are reported.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>5. Write a Short Interpretation Below the Table</h2>

  <p>
    A table should be followed by 1–2 sentences summarizing the key pattern.
  </p>

  <p>
    Example:
  </p>

  <p>
    “Female students show a higher preference for group study (70%) compared to male students (40%).”
  </p>

  <p>
    Do not restate every number. Highlight the key pattern.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>6. Avoid These Reporting Errors</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>No title or unclear title</li>
    <li>Missing sample size</li>
    <li>Unclear percentage type</li>
    <li>Too many decimal places</li>
    <li>Mixing counts and percentages inconsistently</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>7. Decimal and Rounding Guidelines</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Percentages usually 0–1 decimal place</li>
    <li>Totals may not sum to exactly 100% due to rounding</li>
    <li>Be consistent across the table</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>8. Academic vs Business Style</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Academic tables are formal and numbered.</li>
    <li>Business tables focus on clarity and quick insights.</li>
    <li>Both require transparent denominators.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Good reporting builds credibility.
  </p>

</section>
