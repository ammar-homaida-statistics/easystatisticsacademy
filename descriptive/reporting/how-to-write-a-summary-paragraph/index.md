<section class="section">
  <div class="section-head">
    <h1>Writing a Summary Paragraph</h1>
    <p class="lead">
      Statistics are numbers. Reporting is interpretation.  
      A good summary paragraph transforms tables and graphs into a clear, responsible message.
    </p>
  </div>

  <hr>

  <h2>1. What Is a Summary Paragraph?</h2>

  <p>
    A summary paragraph combines:
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>Variable description</li>
    <li>Sample size (n)</li>
    <li>Measure of center</li>
    <li>Measure of spread</li>
    <li>Distribution shape (if relevant)</li>
    <li>Contextual interpretation</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    It answers:
    <strong>“What does this dataset tell us?”</strong>
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>2. Basic Academic Template</h2>

  <p><strong>Template:</strong></p>

  <p style="font-style:italic;">
    “The sample consisted of n = ___ observations. The mean value of ___ was ___ (SD = ___). 
    The distribution was [approximately symmetric / right-skewed / left-skewed]. 
    Values ranged from ___ to ___.”
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>3. Skewed Data Template</h2>

  <p><strong>Template:</strong></p>

  <p style="font-style:italic;">
    “Because the distribution of ___ was right-skewed, the median (___) and interquartile range (___–___) 
    were used as primary summary measures.”
  </p>

  <p>
    Notice: We justify the choice of statistic.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>4. Business Reporting Template</h2>

  <p><strong>Template:</strong></p>

  <p style="font-style:italic;">
    “Average monthly revenue was ___ with moderate variability (SD = ___). 
    Most values clustered between ___ and ___, with a small number of high outliers.”
  </p>

  <p>
    Business summaries emphasize interpretation and clarity.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>5. What Makes a Strong Summary?</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Clear variable identification</li>
    <li>Correct statistics for scale and shape</li>
    <li>Units included</li>
    <li>No causal claims</li>
    <li>No over-interpretation</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>6. Common Weak Reporting</h2>

  ❌ “The average is 45.”

  Problems:
  <ul>
    <li>No unit</li>
    <li>No sample size</li>
    <li>No variability</li>
    <li>No context</li>
  </ul>

  Better:
  <p>
    “The mean exam score was 45 out of 100 (SD = 12; n = 120), indicating substantial variability.”
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>7. Conceptual Rule</h2>

  <p>
    A summary paragraph should allow someone to understand the dataset
    <strong>without seeing the table or graph.</strong>
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>Summary</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Reporting requires structure.</li>
    <li>Always include n, center, spread, and context.</li>
    <li>Match statistics to distribution shape.</li>
    <li>Avoid vague statements like “the average is…”</li>
  </ul>
