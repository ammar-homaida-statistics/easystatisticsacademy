    <h2>Why Tables Matter</h2>

    <p>
      Tables organize information into rows and columns,
      making comparisons easier and reducing the need for lengthy explanations.
    </p>

    <p>
      They are especially useful when readers need precise values rather than visual impressions.
    </p>

    <div class="concept-box">

      <strong>Key idea:</strong>

      <p>
        Tables are best when accuracy and exact numerical values are important.
      </p>

    </div>

    <h2>The Purpose of a Statistical Table</h2>

    <p>
      A statistical table should help readers:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">

      <li>Find information quickly</li>

      <li>Compare values easily</li>

      <li>Understand patterns in the data</li>

      <li>Interpret results correctly</li>

    </ul>

    <p>
      Every element of a table should support these goals.
    </p>

    <h2>Every Table Needs a Title</h2>

    <p>
      A table should always include a clear and informative title.
    </p>

    <p>
      Readers should understand what the table contains without reading the surrounding text.
    </p>

    <div class="example-box">

      <p>
        Poor title:
      </p>

      <p>
        "Results"
      </p>

    </div>

    <div class="example-box">

      <p>
        Better title:
      </p>

      <p>
        "Distribution of Exam Scores for 250 University Students"
      </p>

    </div>

    <h2>Label Rows and Columns Clearly</h2>

    <p>
      Every row and column should have meaningful labels.
    </p>

    <p>
      Readers should never have to guess what a number represents.
    </p>

    <div class="example-box">

      <p>
        Poor label:
      </p>

      <p>
        Value
      </p>

    </div>

    <div class="example-box">

      <p>
        Better label:
      </p>

      <p>
        Monthly Revenue (USD)
      </p>

    </div>

    <h2>Include Units</h2>

    <p>
      Numbers without units are often difficult to interpret.
    </p>

    <p>
      Whenever applicable,
      units should appear in the table title,
      column headings,
      or footnotes.
    </p>

    <div class="example-box">

      <p>
        Age (Years)
      </p>

    </div>

    <div class="example-box">

      <p>
        Income (USD)
      </p>

    </div>

    <p>
      Units prevent misunderstandings and improve clarity.
    </p>

    <h2>Keep Tables Simple</h2>

    <p>
      Tables should contain enough information to be useful,
      but not so much that they overwhelm readers.
    </p>

    <p>
      Unnecessary rows,
      columns,
      and decorative elements should be avoided.
    </p>

    <p>
      Simplicity improves readability.
    </p>

    <h2>Organize Information Logically</h2>

    <p>
      Rows and columns should follow a logical order.
    </p>

    <p>
      Common approaches include:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">

      <li>Alphabetical order</li>

      <li>Numerical order</li>

      <li>Chronological order</li>

      <li>Descending frequency</li>

    </ul>

    <p>
      Consistent ordering helps readers locate information quickly.
    </p>

    <h2>Round Consistently</h2>

    <p>
      Excessive decimal places can distract readers.
    </p>

    <div class="example-box">

      <p>
        42.736198
      </p>

    </div>

    <p>
      is often less useful than:
    </p>

    <div class="example-box">

      <p>
        42.74
      </p>

    </div>

    <p>
      The level of precision should match the purpose of the analysis.
    </p>

    <h2>Align Numbers Properly</h2>

    <p>
      Numerical values should be aligned consistently.
    </p>

    <p>
      Proper alignment makes comparisons easier and improves readability.
    </p>

    <p>
      Decimal alignment is especially helpful when many values contain decimals.
    </p>

    <h2>Example of a Good Table</h2>

    <div class="table-wrap">

      <table>

        <thead>

          <tr>
            <th>Department</th>
            <th>Employees</th>
            <th>Average Salary (USD)</th>
          </tr>

        </thead>

        <tbody>

          <tr>
            <td>Finance</td>
            <td>42</td>
            <td>78,500</td>
          </tr>

          <tr>
            <td>Marketing</td>
            <td>35</td>
            <td>69,200</td>
          </tr>

          <tr>
            <td>Operations</td>
            <td>58</td>
            <td>74,100</td>
          </tr>

        </tbody>

      </table>

    </div>

    <p>
      The table is clearly labeled,
      organized,
      and easy to read.
    </p>

    <h2>Avoid Overcrowding</h2>

    <p>
      Large tables containing dozens of variables can overwhelm readers.
    </p>

    <p>
      Consider splitting complex tables into smaller sections when appropriate.
    </p>

    <p>
      Smaller tables are often easier to understand.
    </p>

    <h2>Use Footnotes When Necessary</h2>

    <p>
      Additional explanations should usually be placed in footnotes rather than inside the table body.
    </p>

    <p>
      Footnotes may explain:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">

      <li>Abbreviations</li>

      <li>Special calculations</li>

      <li>Missing data</li>

      <li>Data sources</li>

    </ul>

    <p>
      This keeps the table clean while providing important context.
    </p>

    <h2>Report Percentages Carefully</h2>

    <p>
      When percentages are included,
      readers should know exactly how they were calculated.
    </p>

    <p>
      Indicate whether percentages are:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">

      <li>Overall percentages</li>

      <li>Row percentages</li>

      <li>Column percentages</li>

    </ul>

    <p>
      Ambiguous percentages can lead to incorrect interpretations.
    </p>

    <h2>Highlight Important Results Sparingly</h2>

    <p>
      Sometimes tables use bold text or emphasis to highlight key findings.
    </p>

    <p>
      This can be effective when used sparingly.
    </p>

    <p>
      Overuse reduces its impact.
    </p>

    <h2>Do Not Force Readers to Calculate</h2>

    <p>
      Important patterns should be explained in accompanying text.
    </p>

    <p>
      Readers should not be expected to discover every conclusion themselves.
    </p>

    <div class="example-box">

      <p>
        Table:
        Mean Score = 82.4
      </p>

      <p>
        Accompanying text:
        "Average performance was relatively high."
      </p>

    </div>

    <p>
      Interpretation complements the table.
    </p>

    <h2>Tables and Summary Paragraphs Work Together</h2>

    <p>
      Tables provide details.
    </p>

    <p>
      Summary paragraphs provide interpretation.
    </p>

    <p>
      Effective reports typically include both.
    </p>

    <h2>Common Table Mistakes</h2>

    <ul class="bullets">

      <li>Missing titles</li>

      <li>Missing units</li>

      <li>Unclear labels</li>

      <li>Inconsistent rounding</li>

      <li>Too many decimal places</li>

      <li>Overcrowded layouts</li>

      <li>No interpretation</li>

    </ul>

    <p>
      Avoiding these mistakes greatly improves communication.
    </p>

    <h2>Checklist for Professional Tables</h2>

    <ul class="bullets">

      <li>Clear title</li>

      <li>Meaningful row and column labels</li>

      <li>Units included</li>

      <li>Logical ordering</li>

      <li>Consistent rounding</li>

      <li>Appropriate use of percentages</li>

      <li>Supporting interpretation in text</li>

    </ul>

    <p>
      Following this checklist will improve most statistical tables.
    </p>

    <h2>Why Good Tables Matter</h2>

    <p>
      Tables remain one of the most widely used tools in research,
      business,
      government,
      healthcare,
      and science.
    </p>

    <p>
      The ability to design and report tables effectively is therefore a fundamental statistical skill.
    </p>

    <h2>Looking Ahead</h2>

    <p>
      Tables communicate exact values,
      but many patterns are easier to see visually.
    </p>

    <p>
      The next lesson focuses on reporting graphs properly and using visualizations to communicate statistical findings effectively.

        <li>Tables are best for presenting precise numerical information</li>

        <li>Units should always be reported when applicable</li>

        <li>Logical organization improves readability</li>

        <li>Consistent rounding prevents confusion</li>

        <li>Interpretation should accompany important tables</li>
