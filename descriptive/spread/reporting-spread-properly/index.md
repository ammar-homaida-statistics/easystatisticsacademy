<section class="section">
  <div class="section-head">
    <h1>Reporting Spread Properly</h1>
    <p class="lead">
      A measure of variability has no meaning unless it is interpreted correctly and reported with context.
      Spread must always be reported together with center.
    </p>
  </div>

  <hr>

  <h2>1. Never Report Spread Alone</h2>

  <p>
    Variability describes dispersion around a central value.
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>Wrong: “Standard deviation = 12.”</li>
    <li>Correct: “Mean = 70, SD = 12.”</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Spread only makes sense relative to a central measure.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>2. Always Include Units</h2>

  <p>
    If data are in kilograms, dollars, or minutes,
    variability must be reported in the same unit.
  </p>

  <p>
    Example:
  </p>

  <p><strong>Mean income = $45,000, SD = $8,000.</strong></p>

  <hr>

  <h2>3. Match Spread to Distribution Shape</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Symmetric distribution → Mean & Standard Deviation</li>
    <li>Skewed distribution → Median & IQR</li>
    <li>Outliers present → Robust measures preferred</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    The reporting choice must reflect data structure.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>4. Sample vs Population</h2>

  <p>
    Clarify whether you are reporting:
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>Population variance (σ²)</li>
    <li>Sample variance (s²)</li>
    <li>Population SD (σ)</li>
    <li>Sample SD (s)</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Academic writing must distinguish them.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>5. Avoid Overinterpretation</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Spread does not imply causality.</li>
    <li>High variability does not mean “bad performance.”</li>
    <li>Low variability does not always mean “consistency” is desirable.</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Interpretation must match the research context.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>6. Business vs Academic Reporting</h2>

  <p><strong>Academic style:</strong></p>
  <p>“The mean score was 72 (SD = 10).”</p>

  <p><strong>Business style:</strong></p>
  <p>“Average monthly sales were 72 units, with moderate variation (SD = 10).”</p>

  <p>
    The structure differs, but transparency is required in both.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>7. Reporting Checklist</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Include a measure of center</li>
    <li>Include a measure of spread</li>
    <li>Specify sample size (n)</li>
    <li>Include units</li>
    <li>Match measure to distribution shape</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>Summary</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Spread is meaningful only with center.</li>
    <li>Units and context are essential.</li>
    <li>Choose robust measures when needed.</li>
    <li>Clarity builds credibility.</li>
  </ul>
