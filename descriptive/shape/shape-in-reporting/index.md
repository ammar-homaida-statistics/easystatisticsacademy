<section class="section">
  <div class="section-head">
    <h1>Reporting Shape Properly</h1>
    <p class="lead">
      Describing center and spread is not enough.
      Good statistical reporting must clearly communicate distribution shape.
    </p>
  </div>

  <hr>

  <h2>1. Why Shape Must Be Reported</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Shape affects interpretation of the mean</li>
    <li>Shape determines robustness of conclusions</li>
    <li>Shape reveals skewness and extreme values</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Without shape, summary statistics can mislead.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>2. Reporting Symmetry</h2>

  <p>Example:</p>

  <p>
    “The distribution appears approximately symmetric around the mean.”
  </p>

  <p>
    Avoid vague phrases like “looks normal” without justification.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>3. Reporting Skewness</h2>

  <p>Correct phrasing:</p>

  <ul>
    <li>“The distribution is moderately right-skewed.”</li>
    <li>“A long right tail suggests extreme high values.”</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    When skewed, explain implications:
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>Mean may exceed median (right skew)</li>
    <li>Median may better represent central tendency</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>4. Reporting Outliers</h2>

  <p>
    Do not hide outliers. Report them clearly.
  </p>

  <p>Example:</p>

  <p>
    “Two extreme values were observed above 120, influencing the mean.”
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>5. Combining Shape With Center and Spread</h2>

  <p>Strong reporting example:</p>

  <p>
    “The distribution is slightly right-skewed (median = 72, mean = 75, SD = 12),
    indicating a few higher extreme values.”
  </p>

  <p>
    This integrates:
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>Center</li>
    <li>Spread</li>
    <li>Shape</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>6. Academic vs Business Reporting</h2>

  <strong>Academic style:</strong>
  <ul>
    <li>Explicit mention of skewness</li>
    <li>Formal statistical terminology</li>
  </ul>

  <strong>Business style:</strong>
  <ul>
    <li>Simpler language</li>
    <li>Focus on practical implications</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>Common Mistakes</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Ignoring skewness entirely</li>
    <li>Calling every distribution “normal”</li>
    <li>Reporting mean without discussing shape</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>Summary</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Shape completes the statistical picture.</li>
    <li>Always interpret skewness and tails.</li>
    <li>Combine center, spread, and shape in reports.</li>
  </ul>
