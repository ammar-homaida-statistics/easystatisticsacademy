  <div class="section-head">
    <h1>The Normal Distribution (Conceptual)</h1>
    <p class="lead">
      The normal distribution is the most important reference distribution in statistics.
      It describes many natural and social phenomena approximately.
    </p>
  </div>

  <hr>

  <h2>1. What Is the Normal Distribution?</h2>

  <p>
    The normal distribution is a continuous, symmetric, bell-shaped distribution.
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>Mean = Median = Mode</li>
    <li>Symmetric around the center</li>
    <li>Tails decrease smoothly</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>2. Why Is It Important?</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Many natural measurements approximate normality</li>
    <li>Sampling distributions tend toward normality (Central Limit Theorem)</li>
    <li>Foundation of many statistical tests</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Even when data are not perfectly normal, the normal distribution is often used as a reference model.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>3. Shape Characteristics</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Perfect symmetry</li>
    <li>No extreme skewness</li>
    <li>Thin, balanced tails</li>
    <li>Defined by two parameters: mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ)</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>4. Mean and Standard Deviation in Normal Data</h2>

  <p>
    In a normal distribution:
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>The mean determines the center</li>
    <li>The standard deviation determines spread</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Changing the mean shifts the curve horizontally.
    Changing the standard deviation changes its width.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>5. Real-World Examples (Approximate)</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Human height</li>
    <li>IQ scores (standardized)</li>
    <li>Measurement error</li>
    <li>Exam scores in large populations</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>6. Important Clarification</h2>

  <p>
    Not all data are normal.
  </p>

  <p>
    Income, waiting times, and many business variables are skewed.
  </p>

  <p>
    Assuming normality without checking can lead to incorrect conclusions.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>Summary</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>The normal distribution is symmetric and bell-shaped.</li>
    <li>It is defined by mean and standard deviation.</li>
    <li>It serves as a central reference in statistics.</li>
    <li>Normality should be assessed, not assumed.</li>
  </ul>
