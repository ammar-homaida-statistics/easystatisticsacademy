<section class="section">

  <div class="section-head">
    <h1>The Empirical Rule (68–95–99.7)</h1>
    <p class="lead">
      In normal distributions, data follow predictable patterns within standard deviation intervals.
    </p>
  </div>

  <hr>

  <h2>1. What Is the Empirical Rule?</h2>

  <p>
    The Empirical Rule describes how data are distributed around the mean in a normal distribution.
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>About 68% of observations lie within 1 standard deviation of the mean.</li>
    <li>About 95% lie within 2 standard deviations.</li>
    <li>About 99.7% lie within 3 standard deviations.</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>2. Mathematical Representation</h2>

  <p>
    If the mean is μ and the standard deviation is σ:
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>μ ± 1σ → ~68%</li>
    <li>μ ± 2σ → ~95%</li>
    <li>μ ± 3σ → ~99.7%</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>3. Why It Matters</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Helps interpret “typical” ranges.</li>
    <li>Identifies unusual values.</li>
    <li>Forms basis for z-scores.</li>
    <li>Used in quality control and risk assessment.</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>4. Example</h2>

  <p>
    Suppose exam scores are normally distributed with:
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>Mean = 70</li>
    <li>Standard deviation = 10</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Then:
  </p>

  <ul>
    <li>68% of students score between 60 and 80.</li>
    <li>95% score between 50 and 90.</li>
    <li>99.7% score between 40 and 100.</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>5. Important Warning</h2>

  <p>
    The Empirical Rule only applies to approximately normal distributions.
  </p>

  <p>
    For skewed or heavy-tailed distributions, these percentages are not accurate.
  </p>

  <hr>

  <h2>6. Conceptual Interpretation</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>Most data cluster near the mean.</li>
    <li>Extreme values are rare.</li>
    <li>The farther from the mean, the lower the probability.</li>
  </ul>

  <hr>

  <h2>Summary</h2>

  <ul>
    <li>The Empirical Rule describes predictable spread in normal data.</li>
    <li>Standard deviation determines interval width.</li>
    <li>Useful for identifying unusual observations.</li>
    <li>Must not be used for non-normal data.</li>
  </ul>
