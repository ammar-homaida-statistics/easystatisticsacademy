<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Why this topic matters</h2>

    <p>
      Experiments are the primary tool for studying
      cause-and-effect relationships.
    </p>

    <p>
      Many claims about effectiveness or impact
      rely on experimental evidence.
    </p>

    <p>
      Understanding experiments clarifies why
      some conclusions are stronger than others.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Manipulation and control</h2>

    <p>
      In an experiment, researchers actively
      manipulate one or more variables.
    </p>

    <p>
      Conditions are controlled so that
      differences in outcomes can be attributed
      to the manipulation.
    </p>

    <p>
      This distinguishes experiments
      from observational studies.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Treatments and outcomes</h2>

    <p>
      A treatment is the condition or intervention
      applied to experimental units.
    </p>

    <p>
      The outcome is the response measured
      after the treatment is applied.
    </p>

    <p>
      Experiments compare outcomes
      across different treatments.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Random assignment</h2>

    <p>
      Random assignment means that experimental units
      are assigned to treatments by chance.
    </p>

    <p>
      This helps balance unknown factors
      across treatment groups.
    </p>

    <p>
      Random assignment is a key reason
      experiments support causal conclusions.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Why experiments are powerful</h2>

    <p>
      By controlling conditions and using
      random assignment, experiments reduce bias.
    </p>

    <p>
      This allows stronger claims about
      cause-and-effect relationships.
    </p>

    <p>
      When feasible and ethical,
      experiments provide the strongest evidence.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section section-soft">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Key idea to remember</h2>

    <p>
      Experiments are powerful because
      they combine control with random assignment.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section section-next">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Simple summary</h2>

    <p>
      Experiments involve manipulating conditions,
      assigning treatments, and measuring outcomes.
      They are designed to support causal conclusions.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>
