<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Why this topic matters</h2>

    <p>
      Binary data appear frequently in real-world datasets,
      especially in surveys, experiments, and classification tasks.
    </p>

    <p>
      Because binary variables take only two values,
      they are often treated as trivial or easy to analyze.
    </p>

    <p>
      This assumption leads to misuse of statistical methods
      and misinterpretation of results.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Binary variables as a special case</h2>

    <p>
      Binary data are variables that take exactly two possible values,
      such as yes or no, success or failure, present or absent.
    </p>

    <p>
      Although they may be stored as numbers,
      binary values represent categories, not quantities.
    </p>

    <p>
      For this reason, binary data require careful interpretation
      despite their simple appearance.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Why binary data are not simple</h2>

    <p>
      Binary variables collapse complex outcomes
      into only two categories.
    </p>

    <p>
      This reduction hides variability and nuance
      present in the underlying phenomenon.
    </p>

    <p>
      Statistical analysis of binary data must therefore
      account for uncertainty and information loss.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Common misuse in analysis</h2>

    <p>
      A frequent error is treating binary data
      as if they were continuous numerical values.
    </p>

    <p>
      Calculations such as means or correlations
      can be misleading when applied without care.
    </p>

    <p>
      Correct analysis depends on recognizing
      what binary values actually represent.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section section-soft">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Key idea to remember</h2>

    <p>
      Binary data are simple in form but not in meaning.
      They require thoughtful statistical treatment.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>
