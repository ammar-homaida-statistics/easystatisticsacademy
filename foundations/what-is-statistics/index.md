<section class="section" id="content">

  <!-- INTUITION -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Intuition</h2>

    <p>
      In real life, we rarely have complete information. Instead, we observe data and try to understand what it tells us about the world.
    </p>

    <p>
      Statistics provides a framework for:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Summarizing data</li>
      <li>Understanding patterns</li>
      <li>Making decisions under uncertainty</li>
      <li>Drawing conclusions about a larger system</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      The key idea is that <strong>data is only a partial and imperfect view of reality</strong>.
      Statistics helps us reason correctly despite that limitation.
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- FORMAL DEFINITION -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Formal Definition</h2>

    <p>
      Statistics is the field concerned with the collection, organization, analysis, interpretation,
      and communication of data, especially in the presence of variability and uncertainty.
    </p>

    <p>
      It combines:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li><strong>Data collection</strong> (how data is obtained)</li>
      <li><strong>Data description</strong> (summaries and visualization)</li>
      <li><strong>Inference</strong> (drawing conclusions beyond the data)</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <!-- CORE IDEA -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>The Core Idea: Variability</h2>

    <p>
      A central concept in statistics is <strong>variability</strong>.
    </p>

    <p>
      Even when measuring the same thing, results are rarely identical.
    </p>

    <p>
      For example:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Heights of people vary</li>
      <li>Test scores vary</li>
      <li>Measurements contain noise</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      Statistics is fundamentally about understanding and modeling this variability.
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- EXAMPLE -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Example</h2>

    <p>
      Suppose we want to know the average height of students in a university.
    </p>

    <p>
      Measuring every student may be impossible, so we take a sample:
    </p>

    <p>
      Sample heights (cm): 170, 175, 168, 180, 172
    </p>

    <p>
      We compute the average:
    </p>

    <p>
      Mean ≈ 173 cm
    </p>

    <p>
      But this is not the exact truth — it is an <strong>estimate</strong>.
    </p>

    <p>
      Statistics helps us answer:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>How accurate is this estimate?</li>
      <li>How much could it vary?</li>
      <li>What can we say about all students?</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <!-- WHY NOT JUST MATH -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Statistics Is Not Just Calculation</h2>

    <p>
      Computing a mean or drawing a graph is not, by itself, statistics.
    </p>

    <p>
      Statistics involves:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Choosing what data to collect</li>
      <li>Understanding limitations of data</li>
      <li>Interpreting results correctly</li>
      <li>Recognizing uncertainty</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      Two people can compute the same number but reach different conclusions
      depending on how they interpret it.
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- COMMON MISTAKES -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Common Misconceptions</h2>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Statistics is just formulas → ❌ It is reasoning under uncertainty</li>
      <li>Statistics gives exact answers → ❌ It gives probabilistic conclusions</li>
      <li>More data always means correct results → ❌ Bias can still exist</li>
      <li>Software does statistics → ❌ Software only computes, it does not think</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <!-- WHY IT MATTERS -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Why This Matters</h2>

    <p>
      Statistics is used in:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Medicine (clinical trials)</li>
      <li>Economics (policy decisions)</li>
      <li>Machine learning (model training)</li>
      <li>Scientific research</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      Without proper statistical thinking, conclusions can be misleading or completely wrong.
    </p>
  </div>
