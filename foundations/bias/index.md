<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Why this topic matters</h2>

    <p>
      Bias affects the validity of conclusions before any analysis begins.
    </p>

    <p>
      Even sophisticated methods cannot correct results
      that are fundamentally biased.
    </p>

    <p>
      Recognizing bias is essential for interpreting data responsibly.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>What bias is</h2>

    <p>
      Bias is a systematic distortion that causes results
      to deviate from the truth.
    </p>

    <p>
      It reflects a consistent direction of error,
      not random fluctuation.
    </p>

    <p>
      Bias can arise from study design, data collection,
      or measurement processes.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Sampling bias</h2>

    <p>
      Sampling bias occurs when some members of the population
      are more likely to be included than others.
    </p>

    <p>
      As a result, the sample does not represent
      the target population accurately.
    </p>

    <p>
      Increasing sample size does not remove sampling bias
      if the sampling process remains flawed.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Measurement bias</h2>

    <p>
      Measurement bias arises when the process of measurement
      consistently overstates or understates values.
    </p>

    <p>
      Poorly worded questions, faulty instruments,
      or systematic reporting errors can introduce bias.
    </p>

    <p>
      Measurement bias affects the data itself,
      not just the analysis.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Selection bias</h2>

    <p>
      Selection bias occurs when inclusion in the study
      depends on factors related to the outcome.
    </p>

    <p>
      This creates distorted relationships
      between variables.
    </p>

    <p>
      Selection bias is common in observational studies
      and voluntary participation.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Why bias is systematic, not random</h2>

    <p>
      Random error varies unpredictably
      from observation to observation.
    </p>

    <p>
      Bias pushes results consistently
      in a particular direction.
    </p>

    <p>
      Because of this, bias does not disappear
      with larger samples.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section section-soft">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Key idea to remember</h2>

    <p>
      Bias is a systematic problem.
      More data alone cannot fix it.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section section-next">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Simple summary</h2>

    <p>
      Bias causes results to systematically differ from the truth.
      It can arise from sampling, measurement, or selection
      and cannot be removed by increasing sample size alone.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>
