<!-- CONTENT -->
<section class="section" id="content">

  <!-- PROBLEM -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>The Problem We Face</h2>

    <p>
      In statistics, we rarely know the truth about a population.
    </p>

    <p>
      Instead, we work with samples.
    </p>

    <p>
      But samples vary.
    </p>

    <p>
      This creates uncertainty.
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- KEY QUESTION -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>The Key Question</h2>

    <p>
      If our data changes from sample to sample:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>How reliable are our conclusions?</li>
      <li>How far might we be from the truth?</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div class="section-block">
  <h2>Conceptual Flow</h2>

  <ul class="bullets">
    <li>Population → unknown truth</li>
    <li>Sample → observed data</li>
    <li>Statistic → estimate</li>
    <li>Sampling variability → uncertainty</li>
    <li>Probability → quantifies uncertainty</li>
  </ul>
</div>

  <!-- WHY NOT DETERMINISTIC -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Why Deterministic Thinking Fails</h2>

    <p>
      In deterministic systems:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Same input → same output</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      In statistics:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Same population → different samples → different results</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      So we cannot rely on exact answers.
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- SOLUTION -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>The Solution: Probability</h2>

    <p>
      Probability gives us a way to describe uncertainty mathematically.
    </p>

    <p>
  In statistics, probability is used to model the variability of sample statistics.
</p>

    <p>
      It allows us to:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Measure how likely outcomes are</li>
      <li>Model variation in data</li>
      <li>Quantify uncertainty in estimates</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div class="section-block">
  <h2>Probability Is Not Certainty</h2>

  <p>
    Probability does not tell us exactly what will happen.
  </p>

  <p>
    It tells us how likely different outcomes are.
  </p>

  <p>
    This allows us to make informed decisions under uncertainty.
  </p>
</div>

  <!-- EXAMPLE -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Example</h2>

    <p>
      Suppose we estimate the average height using a sample.
    </p>

    <p>
      Instead of saying:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>“The true mean is exactly 170 cm” ❌</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      We say:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>“The true mean is likely between 165 and 175 cm” ✅</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      That “likely” is quantified using probability.
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- WHAT PROBABILITY DOES -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>What Probability Enables</h2>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Confidence intervals</li>
      <li>Hypothesis testing</li>
      <li>Error rates (Type I and II errors)</li>
      <li>Modeling randomness</li>
      <li>Understanding sampling distributions</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <!-- CORE IDEA -->
  <div class="section-block">
  <h2>The Core Idea</h2>

  <p>
    Probability connects:
  </p>

  <ul class="bullets">
    <li>Data → variability → uncertainty → conclusions</li>
  </ul>

  <p>
    Without probability, we cannot quantify uncertainty in inference.
  </p>
</div>

  <!-- MISCONCEPTION -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Common Misconception</h2>

    <p>
      Probability is not just about games or randomness.
    </p>

    <p>
      In statistics, it is about:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Uncertainty in data</li>
      <li>Uncertainty in conclusions</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div class="section-block">
  <h2>Key Insight</h2>

  <p>
    Probability turns uncertainty into something we can measure and reason about.
  </p>
</div>

  <!-- BIG IDEA -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>The Big Idea</h2>

    <p>
      Statistics answers questions using data.
    </p>

    <p>
      Probability makes those answers reliable.
    </p>
  </div>
