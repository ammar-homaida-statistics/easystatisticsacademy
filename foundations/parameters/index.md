<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Why this topic matters</h2>

    <p>
      Statistics is ultimately concerned with learning about
      unknown characteristics of populations.
    </p>

    <p>
      Confusing parameters with sample results
      leads to incorrect interpretation and false certainty.
    </p>

    <p>
      Understanding parameters clarifies the goal
      of statistical analysis.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>What parameters are</h2>

    <p>
      A parameter is a numerical characteristic
      of a population.
    </p>

    <p>
      It describes a feature of the entire population,
      not of a particular sample.
    </p>

    <p>
      Parameters exist conceptually,
      even if they are never measured exactly.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Parameters as fixed but unknown</h2>

    <p>
      For a given population, a parameter has
      a single true value.
    </p>

    <p>
      That value does not change from sample to sample.
    </p>

    <p>
      What changes is our information about it,
      not the parameter itself.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Examples of parameters</h2>

    <p>
      Common parameters include the population mean,
      population proportion, and population variance.
    </p>

    <p>
      These values summarize important aspects
      of the population as a whole.
    </p>

    <p>
      Parameters are often denoted using special symbols,
      but their meaning is conceptual rather than mathematical.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Why parameters are not observed</h2>

    <p>
      Observing a parameter would require measuring
      the entire population.
    </p>

    <p>
      As discussed earlier, populations are usually
      unreachable in practice.
    </p>

    <p>
      Statistics exists because parameters cannot
      be directly observed.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section section-soft">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Key idea to remember</h2>

    <p>
      Parameters describe populations.
      They are fixed, but they are unknown.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section section-next">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Simple summary</h2>

    <p>
      A parameter is a numerical characteristic
      of a population. Because populations cannot
      usually be fully observed, parameters remain unknown.
    </p>
