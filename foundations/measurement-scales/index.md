<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Why this topic matters</h2>

    <p>
      Binary variables appear frequently in surveys, experiments,
      medical studies, and classification problems.
    </p>

    <p>
      Because they take only two values, binary data are often
      treated as trivial or easy to analyze.
    </p>

    <p>
      This misunderstanding leads to incorrect summaries,
      inappropriate methods, and misleading conclusions.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Binary variables as a special case</h2>

    <p>
      Binary data consist of variables that take exactly two possible values,
      such as yes or no, success or failure, present or absent.
    </p>

    <p>
      Although binary values are often coded numerically,
      they represent categories rather than quantities.
    </p>

    <p>
      This makes binary variables a special case that sits
      between categorical and numerical data.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Why binary data are not “simple”</h2>

    <p>
      Binary variables compress complex phenomena into two outcomes.
    </p>

    <p>
      This simplification hides variation, uncertainty,
      and underlying structure in the data.
    </p>

    <p>
      Statistical analysis must therefore account for
      information loss and uncertainty.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Common misuse in analysis</h2>

    <p>
      A common mistake is treating binary data as if they were
      continuous numerical measurements.
    </p>

    <p>
      Calculations such as means or correlations can be misleading
      when applied without understanding what the values represent.
    </p>

    <p>
      Correct analysis depends on recognizing that binary values
      encode categories, not magnitudes.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section section-soft">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Key idea to remember</h2>

    <p>
      Binary data are simple in form but not in meaning.
      They require careful statistical interpretation.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>
