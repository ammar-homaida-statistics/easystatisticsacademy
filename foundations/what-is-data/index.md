  <!-- DEFINITION -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Definition of Data</h2>

    <p>
      Data is a collection of observations, measurements, or values
      that represent information about a real-world phenomenon.
    </p>

    <p>
      In simple terms:
      <strong>data is recorded information about something we want to study.</strong>
    </p>

    <p>
      Data typically consists of observations (units) and variables (characteristics measured on those units).
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- EXAMPLES -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Examples of Data</h2>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Heights of students: 170, 165, 180</li>
      <li>Exam scores: 60, 75, 90</li>
      <li>Gender: Male, Female</li>
      <li>Temperature over time: 22°C, 24°C, 19°C</li>
      <li>Customer satisfaction ratings: 1–5</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      These values are not arbitrary — they represent real-world observations.
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- WHY DATA -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Why Data Is Important</h2>

    <p>
      Statistics is entirely based on data.
    </p>

    <p>
      Without data, we cannot:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>summarize information</li>
      <li>detect patterns</li>
      <li>test hypotheses</li>
      <li>make decisions</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      Data is the bridge between:
      <strong>real-world phenomena and statistical conclusions.</strong>
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- RAW VS PROCESSED -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Raw Data vs Processed Data</h2>

    <p>
      Data can appear in two main forms:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li><strong>Raw data</strong> → original, unprocessed values</li>
      <li><strong>Processed data</strong> → summarized or transformed data</li>
    </ul>

    <p><strong>Example:</strong></p>

    <p>Raw data:</p>
    <p>60, 70, 75, 80, 90</p>

    <p>Processed data:</p>
    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Mean = 75</li>
      <li>Range = 30</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      Statistics often works by transforming raw data into meaningful summaries.
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- DATA STRUCTURE -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Data as a Structured Table</h2>

    <p>
      In practice, data is usually organized in a table:
    </p>

    <table style="width:100%; border-collapse:collapse; margin-top:10px;">
      <thead>
        <tr style="background:#f3f4f6;">
          <th style="padding:10px; border:1px solid #ddd;">Student</th>
          <th style="padding:10px; border:1px solid #ddd;">Height</th>
          <th style="padding:10px; border:1px solid #ddd;">Score</th>
        </tr>
      </thead>
      <tbody>
        <tr>
          <td style="padding:10px; border:1px solid #ddd;">A</td>
          <td style="padding:10px; border:1px solid #ddd;">170</td>
          <td style="padding:10px; border:1px solid #ddd;">75</td>
        </tr>
        <tr>
          <td style="padding:10px; border:1px solid #ddd;">B</td>
          <td style="padding:10px; border:1px solid #ddd;">165</td>
          <td style="padding:10px; border:1px solid #ddd;">80</td>
        </tr>
      </tbody>
    </table>

    <p>
      Each row represents an observation, and each column represents a variable.
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- TYPES PREVIEW -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Types of Data (Preview)</h2>

    <p>
      Data can take different forms:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Numerical (e.g., height, income)</li>
      <li>Categorical (e.g., gender, color)</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      These types will be studied in detail later.
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- COMMON MISTAKES -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Common Mistakes</h2>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Thinking data must be numbers only ❌</li>
      <li>Ignoring how data is collected ❌</li>
      <li>Using poor-quality or biased data ❌</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      Good statistics starts with <strong>good data</strong>.
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- CONNECTION -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>What Comes Next?</h2>

    <p>
      Now that we understand what data is, the next question is:

    <ul class="bullets">

      <li>Who or what produces the data?</li>

    <p>
      This leads to the concept of <strong>observational units</strong>.
