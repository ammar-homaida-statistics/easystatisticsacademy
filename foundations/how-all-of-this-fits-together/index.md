<!-- CONTENT -->
<section class="section" id="content">

  <!-- OVERVIEW -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>The Big Picture</h2>

    <p>
      Every statistical analysis follows the same logical flow:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Define the question</li>
      <li>Collect data</li>
      <li>Describe the data</li>
      <li>Make inferences</li>
      <li>Interpret results under uncertainty</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <!-- FLOW -->
  <div class="section-block">
  <h2>The Statistical Process</h2>

  <p>
    Here is the full structure of statistical reasoning:
  </p>

  <ul class="bullets">
    <li>Real-world problem</li>
    <li>→ Define population</li>
    <li>→ Select a sample</li>
    <li>→ Collect data</li>
    <li>→ Assess data quality (bias, missing data, outliers)</li>
    <li>→ Summarize data (descriptive statistics)</li>
    <li>→ Model variability using probability</li>
    <li>→ Draw conclusions (inference)</li>
  </ul>
</div>

  <div class="section-block">
  <h2>The Full Pipeline</h2>

  <ul class="bullets">
    <li>Population → unknown truth</li>
    <li>Sample → observed data</li>
    <li>Statistic → estimate</li>
    <li>Sampling variability → uncertainty</li>
    <li>Probability → quantifies uncertainty</li>
    <li>Inference → conclusions about the population</li>
  </ul>
</div>

  <!-- CORE COMPONENTS -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>The Core Components</h2>

    <h3>1. Data</h3>
    <p>
      Data is the raw material of statistics.
    </p>

    <h3>2. Variables</h3>
    <p>
      Variables define what is being measured.
    </p>

    <h3>3. Population vs Sample</h3>
    <p>
      We want to understand the population but only observe a sample.
    </p>

    <h3>4. Variability</h3>
    <p>
      Different samples produce different results.
    </p>

    <h3>5. Probability</h3>
    <p>
      Probability allows us to quantify that variability.
    </p>

    <h3>6. Inference</h3>
    <p>
      Inference allows us to move from sample to population.
    </p>
  </div>

  <!-- KEY CONNECTION -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>The Key Connection</h2>

    <p>
      The entire logic of statistics can be summarized as:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Data → variability → uncertainty → probability → inference</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      If any part is misunderstood, conclusions become unreliable.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="section-block">
  <h2>The Role of Assumptions</h2>

  <p>
    Every statistical method relies on assumptions.
  </p>

  <p>
    If these assumptions are violated,
    conclusions may be misleading.
  </p>
</div>

  <!-- COMMON FAILURE -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Where Things Go Wrong</h2>

    <p>
      Most statistical mistakes come from breaking this chain:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>Bad data collection</li>
      <li>Ignoring variability</li>
      <li>Misusing probability</li>
      <li>Over-interpreting results</li>
      <li>Ignoring bias and data quality issues</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <!-- MENTAL MODEL -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Your Mental Model</h2>

    <p>
      When you see any statistical result, always ask:
    </p>

    <ul class="bullets">
      <li>What is the population?</li>
      <li>What is the sample?</li>
      <li>What data was collected?</li>
      <li>How much variability is there?</li>
      <li>What uncertainty exists?</li>
      <li>What assumptions are being made?</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

<div class="section-block">
  <h2>One-Line Summary</h2>

  <p>
    Statistics uses data, variability, and probability
    to make informed decisions under uncertainty.
  </p>
</div>

  <!-- TRANSITION -->
  <div class="section-block">

    <ul class="bullets">

      <p>
  Next, we move deeper into statistical methods and tools
  that allow us to apply these ideas in practice.

  </div>

  <div class="section-block">
  <h2>Key Insight</h2>

  <p>
    Statistics is a chain of reasoning.
  </p>

  <p>
    Weakness at any step — data, sampling, or assumptions —
    leads to unreliable conclusions.
  </p>
</div>

  <!-- FINAL MESSAGE -->
  <div class="section-block">
    <h2>Final Insight</h2>

    <p>
      Statistics is not about formulas.
    </p>

    <p>
      It is about reasoning under uncertainty using data.
    </p>
