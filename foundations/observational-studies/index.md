<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Why this topic matters</h2>

    <p>
      Many real-world datasets come from observational studies
      rather than controlled experiments.
    </p>

    <p>
      Conclusions drawn from observational data
      are often misunderstood or overstated.
    </p>

    <p>
      Understanding the limits of observational studies
      helps prevent incorrect causal claims.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>What observational studies are</h2>

    <p>
      An observational study collects data
      without intervening in the system being studied.
    </p>

    <p>
      Researchers observe what naturally occurs
      rather than assigning treatments.
    </p>

    <p>
      The goal is to describe patterns and relationships
      present in existing data.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Passive data collection</h2>

    <p>
      Observational studies rely on passive data collection.
    </p>

    <p>
      This means outcomes and exposures
      are not controlled by the researcher.
    </p>

    <p>
      Data may come from records, surveys,
      or naturally occurring processes.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Lack of control</h2>

    <p>
      Because researchers do not assign conditions,
      many influencing factors remain uncontrolled.
    </p>

    <p>
      Confounding variables may affect
      both predictors and outcomes.
    </p>

    <p>
      This limits the conclusions
      that can be drawn from the data.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Association vs causation</h2>

    <p>
      Observational studies can identify associations
      between variables.
    </p>

    <p>
      An association does not imply
      that one variable causes the other.
    </p>

    <p>
      Establishing causation generally requires
      stronger study designs.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Typical use cases</h2>

    <p>
      Observational studies are common
      in public health, economics, and social science.
    </p>

    <p>
      They are often used when experiments
      would be unethical or impractical.
    </p>

    <p>
      Observational data can generate hypotheses
      and inform further study.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section section-soft">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Key idea to remember</h2>

    <p>
      Observational studies reveal associations,
      not definitive causal relationships.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>

<section class="section section-next">
  <div class="content-narrow">
    <h2>Simple summary</h2>

    <p>
      Observational studies collect data without intervention.
      They are useful for identifying patterns,
      but they have limited ability to establish causation.
    </p>
  </div>
</section>
