<section class="section">

&nbsp; <div class="content-narrow">

&nbsp;   <h2>Why this topic matters</h2>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     Statistics does not begin with formulas or calculations. It begins with asking the right kind of question.

&nbsp;   </p>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     Many errors in data analysis occur because a non-statistical question is treated as if it were statistical.

&nbsp;     When this happens, the analysis may look “scientific” but the reasoning is wrong from the start.

&nbsp;   </p>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     Understanding statistical questions helps you recognize when statistics is needed, what data must be collected,

&nbsp;     and what kind of answer is realistically possible.

&nbsp;   </p>

&nbsp; </div>

</section>



<section class="section">

&nbsp; <div class="content-narrow">

&nbsp;   <h2>What makes a question statistical?</h2>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     A statistical question is a question that cannot be answered with a single fixed value.

&nbsp;   </p>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     It anticipates variability in outcomes and requires multiple observations to be answered.

&nbsp;     Because results vary, the answer is usually expressed as a distribution, a range, or a summary with uncertainty.

&nbsp;   </p>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     Statistical questions focus on patterns and variation rather than exact outcomes.

&nbsp;   </p>

&nbsp; </div>

</section>



<section class="section">

&nbsp; <div class="content-narrow">

&nbsp;   <h2>Questions that require data</h2>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     Statistical questions require data because the answer is not known in advance.

&nbsp;   </p>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     Data must be collected, observed, or measured to understand how outcomes vary across people, time, places,

&nbsp;     or conditions.

&nbsp;   </p>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     Without data, there is nothing to summarize, compare, or reason from statistically.

&nbsp;   </p>

&nbsp; </div>

</section>



<section class="section">

&nbsp; <div class="content-narrow">

&nbsp;   <h2>Questions that require uncertainty</h2>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     A key feature of statistical questions is uncertainty.

&nbsp;   </p>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     Even after data are collected, the answer is not perfectly known because data are limited and variable.

&nbsp;     Good statistical answers communicate uncertainty instead of hiding it.

&nbsp;   </p>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     This is why statistics often uses concepts like variability, error, confidence, and probability.

&nbsp;   </p>

&nbsp; </div>

</section>



<section class="section">

&nbsp; <div class="content-narrow">

&nbsp;   <h2>Non-statistical questions disguised as statistical</h2>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     Some questions look statistical because they mention numbers or data, but they are not statistical questions.

&nbsp;   </p>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     If a question has a single exact answer that does not vary, then statistics is not needed.

&nbsp;     For example, asking for a fixed count or a known value is not statistical.

&nbsp;   </p>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     A useful test is simple: if repeated observations could reasonably produce different values, the question is

&nbsp;     statistical. If not, it is deterministic.

&nbsp;   </p>

&nbsp; </div>

</section>



<section class="section">

&nbsp; <div class="content-narrow">

&nbsp;   <h2>Examples of statistical questions</h2>



&nbsp;   <ul class="bullets">

&nbsp;     <li>What is the average response time of a website under normal usage?</li>

&nbsp;     <li>How do exam scores vary across different classes?</li>

&nbsp;     <li>What proportion of customers are satisfied with a product?</li>

&nbsp;     <li>How does daily temperature vary over a month?</li>

&nbsp;   </ul>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     These questions require multiple observations and involve variability, so they cannot be answered from a single

&nbsp;     measurement.

&nbsp;   </p>

&nbsp; </div>

</section>



<section class="section section-soft">

&nbsp; <div class="content-narrow">

&nbsp;   <h2>Key idea to remember</h2>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     A question is statistical if it involves variability and uncertainty. If the answer is fixed and does not vary,

&nbsp;     statistics is not required.

&nbsp;   </p>

&nbsp; </div>

</section>

<section class="section section-next">

&nbsp; <div class="content-narrow">

&nbsp;   <h2>Simple summary</h2>



&nbsp;   <p>

&nbsp;     Statistical questions are questions where answers vary and cannot be known with certainty from one observation.

&nbsp;     They require data and must account for uncertainty.

&nbsp;   </p>
